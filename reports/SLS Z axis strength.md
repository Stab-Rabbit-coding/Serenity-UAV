# Carbon fibre leaves PA CF weak across layers

The 0.5 Z knockdown the Serenity UAV applies to 3D Systems DuraForm PA CF is **non-conservative in the build axis for both strength and modulus**, and modulus is the worse of the two. 3D Systems publishes only one non-directional value set for DuraForm PA CF in datasheet 3DS-30108A: **12,200 psi (84 MPa) tensile strength, 1,230 ksi (8,500 MPa) modulus and 3.8 % elongation (ASTM D638 Type I)**. No Z data exist from 3D Systems or from any peer-reviewed study of a 30–35 % carbon-fibre PA12. The closest proxy is ALM PA 603-CF on the EOS P3 NEXT. It is also a 33 % carbon-fibre PA12, and its X values match 3DS-30108A almost exactly. Its ASTM D638 build-axis values are **5,370 psi (37 MPa) and 435 ksi (3,000 MPa)**, which are **0.44 of X for strength and 0.35 of X for modulus** (ESTIMATE). A Protolabs UK carbon-filled PA12 sheet independently gives 0.53 and 0.35. Carbon fibre also makes the material anisotropic within the layer plane, which the repository does not yet account for. The cross-recoat (Y) direction keeps only **0.49–0.53 of X modulus on the proxy and 0.41 on the Protolabs sheet**. A part whose in-plane direction is not controlled is therefore also below 0.5 in stiffness. This report recommends sourced ratios for three cases, which are summarised in the final section. Every ratio is an estimate transferred from materials that are *not* DuraForm PA CF. Tensile-to-flexural transfer is unsupported, and only coupons printed on the production machine can qualify the design.

## 3DS-30108A gives one best-direction value set and no orientation

The DuraForm PA CF datasheet (doc 3DS-30108A, dated 10-23, US-EN and A4 variants) has no X, Y, Z or orientation column. It says the material "did not yield" in tension. It still carries the line "Complete data set will be available in Q1 2024" ([3D Systems DuraForm PA CF datasheet, A4](https://www.3dsystems.com/sites/default/files/2023-11/3d-systems-duraform-pa-cf-datasheet-us-a4-2023-10-31-a_web52.pdf); [US-EN variant](https://www.3dsystems.com/sites/default/files/2023-11/3d-systems-duraform-pa-cf-datasheet-us-en-2023-10-31-a_web65.pdf)). No later revision was found. The material page carries only qualitative claims ("33% carbon fiber filled nylon 12") ([3D Systems DuraForm PA CF](https://www.3dsystems.com/materials/duraform-pa-cf-sls)). The launch release names the SLS 380 as the platform and gives no mechanical data ([3D Systems Formnext 2024](https://www.3dsystems.com/press-releases/3d-systems-empowers-innovation-transforms-industries-new-products-formnext-2024)). The support.3dsystems.com documentation page was blocked during research and remains unchecked.

| DuraForm PA CF, 3DS-30108A | Standard | Value | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ultimate tensile strength | ASTM D638 Type I | 12,200 psi (84 MPa) | verified |
| Tensile modulus | ASTM D638 Type I | 1,230 ksi (8,500 MPa) | verified |
| Elongation at break | ASTM D638 Type I | 3.8 % | verified |
| Flexural strength | ASTM D790 | 19,700 psi (140 MPa) | verified |
| Flexural modulus | ASTM D790 | 1,010 ksi (7,000 MPa) | verified |
| Density | ASTM D792 | 1.11 g/cm³ | verified |

These figures should be read as **best-direction (X) values**, not as allowables. The reason is a numerical match rather than a statement from 3D Systems: two independent 33 % CF-PA12 sheets that do report direction give X values of 12,330 psi (85 MPa) and 1,204–1,247 ksi (8,300–8,600 MPa), within about 2 % of 3DS-30108A. The repository already records the 84 MPa and 8,500 MPa figures as "XY (datasheet gives no Z)" in `current-specification/allowables.json`, with the Z entry null. The 0.5 knockdown appears in `airframe/WBS.md` (items OD-19, MS-SLS-PACF and SLS-WALL-STIFF) as an explicit assumption.

## Two 33 % carbon-fibre PA12 sheets agree: Z keeps about a third of the stiffness

EOS's process datasheet for ALM PA 603-CF on the P3 NEXT is the only manufacturer document found that gives X, Y and Z values for a carbon-fibre SLS PA12 under both ISO 527 and ASTM D638 ([EOS PDS ALM PA 603-CF](https://www.eos.info/polymer-solutions/data-sheets/pds-alm-pa-603-cf), last changed 10.11.2026). The sheet describes the powder as "33% Carbon-Fiber-Filled Nylon 12" and does not say whether the 33 % is by weight or volume. DuraForm PA CF is specified against ASTM D638, so the ASTM rows are the primary proxy. Protolabs UK's "PA12 Carbon Filled Black" datasheet corroborates them ([Protolabs UK PA12 Carbon Filled Black](https://www.protolabs.com/media/0okiybqf/pa12-carbon-filled-black-en-14_11_24.pdf)). That sheet is tested to DIN EN ISO 527 and says the carbon-fibre filler "provides different mechanical properties based on the considered three axis direction". Protolabs UK runs 3D Systems sPro machines, but it does not identify its powder as DuraForm PA CF. Its density (1.2 g/cm³) and X elongation (3.2 %) differ from the DuraForm sheet.

All Y/X and Z/X ratios are ESTIMATE (computed from the published means).

Three findings come out of the table. First, **the Z modulus ratio is the most consistent number in the data set**. Three independent rows give 0.35, 0.35 and 0.36, so a 0.5 assumption overstates build-axis stiffness by about 43 % (ESTIMATE). Second, **Z strength scatters more, from 0.44 to 0.53**, and the ASTM row, which matches the DuraForm test method, is the lowest. Third, the in-plane split is large: Y modulus is only 0.41–0.53 of X. Fibres lie in the layer plane and, by inference, along the spreading direction, so "XY" is not one isotropic value for this class of powder. Neither sheet states which machine axis X is. Mapping X to the recoat direction is an inference. It is consistent with CT data showing that about half the fibres in SLS fibre-filled polyamide align with the recoat direction and only about 0.18 align with Z ([Heckner et al. 2020](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/jcs/jcs-04-00108/article_deploy/jcs-04-00108.pdf), verified, PA6/glass, ISO 527-2). The two proxies also come from different machines: the EOS P3 NEXT for ALM, and sPro-class machines for Protolabs. The SLS 380 recoater may align fibres differently.

## Glass beads stay near-isotropic; carbon fibre is what creates the anisotropy

Comparable filled and unfilled SLS polyamides show the fibre is the cause. On EOS's own sheets, all ISO 527 dry and all verified, PA 3200 GF (PA12 with 40 wt % glass beads) keeps **6,820 psi (47 MPa) Z strength, 0.92 of XY, and 363 ksi (2,500 MPa) Z modulus, 0.78** ([EOS MDS PA 3200 GF](https://www.eos.info/en-us/polymer-solutions/polymer-materials/data-sheets/mds-pa-3200-gf?topdf)). Unfilled PA 2200 (PA12) keeps 0.88 of its strength and 1.00 of its 239 ksi (1,650 MPa) modulus ([EOS MDS PA 2200](https://www.eos.info/en-us/polymer-solutions/polymer-materials/data-sheets/mds-pa-2200?topdf)). PA 1101 (PA11) keeps 0.96 and 1.00 ([EOS MDS PA 1101](https://www.eos.info/en-us/polymer-solutions/polymer-materials/data-sheets/mds-pa-1101?topdf)). Every ratio here is an ESTIMATE. In absolute terms, the ALM 33 % carbon-fibre powder's Z strength of 5,370–6,090 psi (37–42 MPa; Protolabs 6,530 psi, 45 MPa) is **no better than unfilled PA12's 6,090 psi (42 MPa) and below the glass-bead grade's 6,820 psi (47 MPa)**. Carbon fibre buys in-plane performance and nothing across layers. The proxy's Z modulus of 421–435 ksi (2,900–3,000 MPa) is still about 1.8× that of unfilled PA12, because some fibres do tilt out of plane.

3D Systems' other filled grades do not help with direction. DuraForm ProX GF (45 MPa, 3,720 MPa, 2.8 %, ASTM D638) and DuraForm HST Composite (48–51 MPa, 5,475–5,725 MPa, 4.5 %, ASTM D638) are published as single values without orientation, all verified ([DuraForm ProX GF datasheet](https://www.3dsystems.com/sites/default/files/2020-07/3d-systems-duraform-prox-gf-plastic-sls-datasheet-usen-2020-06-10-web.pdf); [DuraForm HST datasheet](https://www.3dsystems.com/sites/default/files/2020-07/3d-systems-duraform-hst-datasheet-usen-2020-06-11-web.pdf)). The HST sheet nonetheless calls the material anisotropic "just like fiber-filled" moulded parts. Other carbon-filled SLS powders could not be checked at the manufacturer and remain **unverified**. For ALM PA 640-GSL (glass plus carbon), a Shapeways page reportedly gives a Z value of 33 MPa and 1,945 MPa under ASTM D638 ([Shapeways](https://www.shapeways.com/materials/carbon-reinforced-lightweight-nylon-pa12)); this is unverified, and the page was not opened. No Z value was found for Windform XT 2.0, which has an X strength of about 84 MPa under ISO 527-1, also unverified ([Windform XT 2.0](https://www.windform.com/top-line/windform-xt-2-0/)). Protolabs reports PA 614-GS (40 % glass, ASTM D638) at a Z/XY ratio of 0.91 for strength and 1.00 for modulus, also unverified ([Protolabs PA12 40% Glass-Filled](https://www.protolabs.com/services/3d-printing/plastic/nylon/pa12-40-glass-filled/)). No CF-PA11 Z data were verified.

## Peer-reviewed studies confirm the trend but contain no commercial 33 % CF-PA12

No peer-reviewed study of DuraForm PA CF or any 30–35 % CF-PA12 was found. The literature that does exist agrees with the datasheets once the comparison baseline is matched. Most papers divide Z by an in-plane average, so their numbers should be read against the proxies' **Z/Y ratios of 0.62–0.82 for strength and 0.69–0.85 for modulus** (ESTIMATE; ALM ASTM and ISO, then Protolabs), not against Z/X.

Slager et al. (2025) studied PA12 dry-blended with milled recycled carbon fibre on a custom 5 W diode-laser printer, using ASTM D638 Type V specimens. Z/horizontal tensile strength fell from **0.90 with no fibre to 0.61 at 2.5 vol % and 0.64 at 5 vol %**. Modulus ratios were 0.91, 0.82 and 0.75 (ESTIMATE, computed from the means in their Table 4, which were verified in the full text). At 10 vol %, which is only about 15 wt %, most vertical specimens broke along a layer during depowdering ([Slager et al. 2025](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/polymers/polymers-17-02028/article_deploy/polymers-17-02028.pdf)). The authors attribute the loss to unbonded fibre–fibre contacts that act as voids. Zárybnická et al. (2022) tested on an EOS P396 to ISO 527-2 with n = 10. The glass-plus-carbon ALM PA 640-GSL gave a Z strength of **3,840 psi (26.5 MPa)**, a ratio of 0.82 for strength and 0.84 for modulus. This was the lowest Z strength of the five powders tested. PA 3200 GF came out at 1.00/0.95 and PA 2200 at 0.90/1.00 (all verified; ratios ESTIMATE) ([Zárybnická et al. 2022](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/polymers/polymers-14-01172/article_deploy/polymers-14-01172.pdf)). The same study found **2.7–6× higher Z scatter** than in-plane scatter for PA2200. Pilipović et al. (2024) found that changing hatch distance alone moved PA12 tensile strength from 46 to 8 MPa under ISO 527 ([Pilipović et al. 2024](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/applsci/applsci-14-00976/article_deploy/applsci-14-00976.pdf)). Secondary sources cited there, unverified, report that Z properties are more sensitive to energy input than XY. Taken together, this confirms that Z values depend on the process and the individual machine.

Flexural data by orientation are weaker still. **No manufacturer publishes Z flexural data for any filled SLS polyamide**, DuraForm PA CF included. For unfilled PA12, Baba (2022) measured an upright/flatwise flexural ratio of 0.29 for strength and 0.74 for modulus (ISO 178, Formlabs Fuse 1, verified) ([Baba 2022](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/polymers/polymers-14-01026/article_deploy/polymers-14-01026.pdf)). Earlier DuraForm PA studies cited in that paper report 0.96–1.06 (unverified, secondary). A spread of 0.29 to 1.06 for the *unfilled* polymer means **tensile Z ratios cannot be transferred to the 19,700 psi (140 MPa) / 1,010 ksi (7,000 MPa) flexural values with any confidence**. In elongation, Z failure is brittle and flaw-controlled. Slager measured 4.9 % vertical against 52.9 % horizontal for neat PA12, and 2.6–2.7 % vertical with fibre. Baba describes the failure mode shifting from ductile to "quasi-brittle" in upright bars.

## Recommended design ratios for DuraForm PA CF replace the single 0.5

The ratios below are applied to the 3DS-30108A values. The rule is to take **the lower of the ALM PA 603-CF ASTM D638 ratio and the Protolabs UK ISO 527 ratio for each property**. Case (a) uses X. Case (b) uses the in-plane minimum, which is Y for strength and modulus. Case (c) uses the minimum over all directions, which is Z for strength and modulus. For elongation the rule is applied as an absolute floor: the lowest elongation either proxy reports in the governing directions, divided by 3.8 %. Every entry is an **ESTIMATE**. Neither proxy is DuraForm PA CF. The values are means, not A- or B-basis allowables, and the Protolabs ±400 MPa band puts the Z modulus at roughly 0.30 at one standard deviation.

| Case | Property | Ratio (ESTIMATE) | Design value (ESTIMATE) | Governing source | Repo 0.5 is… |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| (a) load along recoat (X) | Strength | 1.00 | 12,200 psi (84 MPa), ASTM D638 | ALM PA 603-CF ASTM 85/85; Protolabs 85/85 | conservative (by 2.0×) |
| | Modulus | 1.00 | 1,230 ksi (8,500 MPa), ASTM D638 | ALM ASTM 8,600/8,600; Protolabs 8,300/8,300 | conservative (by 2.0×) |
| | Elongation | 0.84 | 3.2 % | Protolabs X 3.2 % (ISO 527) | no repo ratio |
| (b) in-plane, direction uncontrolled (use Y) | Strength | 0.65 | 7,920 psi (54.6 MPa) | Protolabs Y/X 55/85 (ISO 527); ALM ASTM 0.71 | conservative (0.5 vs 0.65) |
| | Modulus | 0.41 | 505 ksi (3,490 MPa) | Protolabs Y/X 3,400/8,300 (ISO 527); ALM ASTM 0.49 | **non-conservative** (0.5 vs 0.41, +22 %) |
| | Elongation | 0.79 | 3.0 % | Protolabs Y 3.0 % (ISO 527) | no repo ratio |
| (c) orientation not fixed, or load across layers (use Z) | Strength | 0.44 | 5,360 psi (37 MPa) | ALM PA 603-CF ASTM D638 Z/X 37/85; Protolabs 0.53 | **non-conservative** (0.5 vs 0.44, +14 %) |
| | Modulus | 0.35 | 431 ksi (2,980 MPa) | ALM ASTM 3,000/8,600; Protolabs 2,900/8,300 (both 0.35) | **non-conservative** (0.5 vs 0.35, +43 %) |
| | Elongation | 0.58 | 2.2 % | Protolabs Z 2.2 % (ISO 527); ALM ASTM 3.0 % | no repo ratio |

In case (a), the 0.5 is conservative only if the part's X axis really lies along the SLS 380's recoat direction. That mapping is inferred, not stated by 3D Systems, EOS or Protolabs, and it must be written on the drawing together with the build orientation. Case (b) is the trap. The repository's "XY fixed" option in OD-19 treats a flat build as worth the full datasheet modulus. A flat skin whose in-plane axis is not specified gets only about 0.41 of that modulus, which is below the 0.5 the repository uses for its *worst* case.

For case (c), the bending-stiffness finding in SLS-WALL-STIFF scales directly with E. If the repository's figure of 52 % of CF-PETG stiffness for a 2.0 mm skin (computed at 0.5) is taken at face value, re-running it at 0.35 gives about **36 %** (ESTIMATE). Stiffness parity then needs about **0.110 in (2.8 mm)** instead of 2.5 mm (ESTIMATE, t ∝ E^-1/3). The CF-PETG baseline itself was not re-verified for this report. For flexural strength and modulus, the provisional practice (ESTIMATE) is to check bending members against the case-(c) *tensile* values above, not to apply ratios to the 140 MPa / 7,000 MPa flexural figures, because no flexural Z data exist for any filled SLS polyamide. Brittle behaviour means a strain check at no more than 2.2 % belongs alongside the stress check for any Z-loaded feature.

## Conclusion

The data change the repository's problem from "which knockdown" to "which direction". DuraForm PA CF is effectively a three-axis orthotropic material, X ≫ Y > Z, and a single scalar cannot be both safe and efficient. Stiffness is where the 0.5 assumption fails most, in Z and also in the uncontrolled in-plane case, so the SLS skin, collar and pod stiffness analyses are the first items to re-run. Strength in X and in-plane is better than the repository assumes, which gives back margin wherever drawings fix orientation relative to the recoater. This makes recoat-direction callouts on drawings worth as much as the thickness increases.

The recommended ratios rest on two datasheets for materials that are not DuraForm PA CF. They come from different machines, do not identify the recoat axis, and report means only. No flexural Z data exist for any filled SLS polyamide. The only qualifying data remain the X, Y and Z ASTM D638 Type I and D790 coupons already planned in `docs/MATSPEC_SLS_PA_CF.md`. They should be printed on the production SLS 380, in the same job and at the same build height as flight parts, and in enough numbers to cover the 2.7–6× Z scatter. Until those results arrive, the case (a), (b) and (c) ratios above should replace the 0.5, and every drawing should state which case it uses.
