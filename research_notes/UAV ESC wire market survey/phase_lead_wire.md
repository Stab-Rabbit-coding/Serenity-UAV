# Phase lead wire (12 AWG / 14 AWG) for 64 mm EDF BLDC motors

Application: three ESC-to-motor phase leads per motor, ~36 A RMS hover / ~47 A RMS full throttle, 6-10 in (150-250 mm) long, routed through a printed nylon conduit beside a 64 mm EDF, in a hot location near the motor.

**Method and access limitations (read first).** In this session the direct page fetch tool could not reach `qx-motor.co` or `remingtonindustries.com` (DNS blocked by the session network allowlist: "getaddrinfo ENOTFOUND"). Following the session rule, I did not try to fetch more hosts after those two failures. All figures below come from web-search result extracts of the cited pages. They were not read directly off the PDF or page by me. Each row's status column says whether the cited page is the manufacturer's own page or datasheet, or a distributor or seller listing. **Re-verify every number against the manufacturer PDF before it goes into a specification.** To fetch the pages directly, `qx-motor.co`, `www.remingtonindustries.com`, `www.daburn.com`, `www.alphawire.com`, `media.iewc.com` and `www.te.com` must be allowed in the session network settings.

## Which manufacturers publish datasheets for fine-strand 12 AWG and 14 AWG silicone or high-temperature fluoropolymer wire, and what are their actual ODs?

### Takeaway
Three US manufacturers publish datasheets for wire that fits this use. Daburn's 2617 (UL 3212 silicone, 150 °C) is 0.165 in (4.20 mm) OD at 14 AWG and 0.183 in (4.66 mm) at 12 AWG. Alpha Wire's PTFE parts (AS22759/11, 200 °C, silver-plated) are much thinner: 0.090 in (2.29 mm) at 14 AWG and 0.111 in (2.82 mm) at 12 AWG. TE Raychem Spec 55 (cross-linked ETFE) is about 0.103 in (2.62 mm) at 12 AWG. None of these US industrial parts is truly "fine strand": they use 19 to 65 strands. The 400- and 680-strand hobby silicone wire (0.138 in (3.5 mm) / 0.177 in (4.5 mm) OD) is sold under Chinese or importer brands with no manufacturer datasheet. I found no Remington Industries silicone wire.

### Inferences
- OD by family. The fluoropolymer parts (AS22759/11 PTFE, Spec 55 ETFE) are about 0.090-0.111 in (2.3-2.8 mm), less than half the cross-section of UL 3212 silicone (0.165-0.190 in, 4.2-4.8 mm). Hobby 680-strand silicone is 0.177 in (4.5 mm) at 12 AWG. For a conduit carrying three leads, PTFE or ETFE 12 AWG takes much less space than 14 AWG silicone.
- Temperature. UL 3212 silicone is rated only 150 °C. If a 200 °C silicone is needed near the motor, the candidates are Daburn 2616 (UL 3135) or 2627 (UL 3231, braided), or the unverified hobby 200 °C claims. Alpha's PTFE is 200 °C.
- Flexibility. US industrial silicone uses 41/30 or 65/30 strands, much coarser than hobby 0.08 mm (about 40 AWG) strands. PTFE 19-strand is stiffer still. For a fixed run in a conduit, flex life matters less than in a flexing lead, but bend radius at the motor and ESC ends still matters.
- Current. 12 AWG (3.31 mm²) has about 1.6x the conductor area of 14 AWG (2.08 mm²); these are standard AWG values, not taken from the cited sources. Over a 150-250 mm run, I2R loss and temperature rise depend on the insulation's temperature class and on cooling. The ampacity figures in the sources are seller "bear current" claims (e.g. "30 A" for 12 AWG on one listing), not engineering ratings, so the ampacity must be calculated rather than taken from listings.
