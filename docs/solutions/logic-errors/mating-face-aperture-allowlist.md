# Mesh-repair pipelines silently re-cap intentional open fuselage mating faces

## Problem

The fuselage hull is built from four shell STLs — cargo, head, middle, rear —
joined at three internal bonded splice collars (generated by
`airframe/stls/fuselage/generate_conforming_collars.py`). Each shell has flat
"mating face" apertures at its joint stations (`head_aft`, `cargo_fwd`,
`cargo_aft`, `middle_fwd`, `middle_aft`, `rear_fwd`; the (Y, keep_sign) pairs
live in the `MATING_PLANES` dict at
`airframe/blender-scripts/add_structural_features.py:398-411`). These
apertures must be genuinely open bores — not solid caps — for a collar to
slip in and bond.

Three separate pipelines each own a subset of these faces, and each ends its
own run with an internal watertight-repair/finalize pass:

- `airframe/blender-scripts/merge_cargo_interior.py` owns `cargo_fwd` /
  `cargo_aft`, finalized inside `finalize_watertight()` (called from
  `repair_exported()`, `merge_cargo_interior.py:1707`).
- `airframe/blender-scripts/add_structural_features.py` owns `head_aft`
  (via `process_head`) and `middle_fwd`/`middle_aft` (via `process_middle`),
  both invoked from the per-shell loop in `main()`.
- `airframe/blender-scripts/regen_rear_interior.py` owns `rear_fwd`, closed
  by a plain CSG half-space subtraction (`out = from_manifold(to_manifold(shell) - neg)`,
  `regen_rear_interior.py:228`) via `manifold3d`.

Each pipeline's internal repair step — `trimesh.repair.fill_holes()` in the
cargo pipeline's case, or (for the rear pipeline) the mathematical fact that a
CSG half-space subtraction always synthesizes a new closed face at the cut
plane — cannot distinguish an intentional open mating aperture from a
genuine boundary-edge mesh defect. It silently re-caps the aperture with a
thin (~0.5-1.0 mm) membrane.

The key diagnostic insight: `trimesh`'s `mesh.is_watertight` is **not**
sufficient to detect this. A hollow tube open at both ends is topologically
genus-1 — the outer wall and inner wall connect at the open rim — and reports
`is_watertight = True`, identically to a solid disk sealing the tube shut.
`tools/open_mating_faces.py` documents this directly:

```python
# Boolean of closed manifolds ⇒ still watertight (an open-ended thick tube is
# watertight like a length of pipe — genus-1, not genus-0: the outer and inner
# walls connect at the open rim instead of each being capped separately).
# is_watertight()==True is therefore NOT sufficient on its own to prove a face
# is genuinely open — is_capped() below (a point probe just inside the face)
# is the real test; a solid disk cap is also watertight.
```
(`tools/open_mating_faces.py:63-68`)

The actual test needed is a point-containment probe a fraction of a
millimeter inboard of the mating face — `mesh.contains()` at that point
returns `True` only if solid material is present there, i.e. the face is
capped.

## Symptoms

Reported by the project owner after opening the FreeCAD assembly of the
internal splice-collar geometry:

- A visible gap on the port side of the cargo/middle mating ring.
- The entire starboard side of that same ring fully blocked off.
- The head/cargo collar's profile tracing a straight vertical line on the
  port side instead of following the hull's actual curve.

Underlying (not directly visible, found during diagnosis): 4 of the 6
mating-face apertures — `cargo_fwd`, `cargo_aft`, `middle_aft`, `rear_fwd` —
were capped by a thin membrane despite each owning pipeline's own
`verify()`/`PASS` check reporting success, because those checks only assert
`is_watertight`, never `is_capped`.

## What Didn't Work

1. **First hypothesis: a `lap_profile()`/`inner_cavity()` heuristic bug.**
   `generate_conforming_collars.py`'s collar generator picks the
   second-largest section loop at each sampled station as the inner cavity
   (`inner_cavity()`, `airframe/stls/fuselage/generate_conforming_collars.py`
   — see the "2nd-largest section loop" logic also mirrored in
   `tools/open_mating_faces.py:175-186`). The hypothesis was that nearby
   structural features (LG-bay bosses, CF-thwart pockets) were producing
   multiple competing loops and confusing this heuristic. This was
   **partially true and remains a real, separate, still-open issue** — see
   "Not resolved" below — but it did not explain the full extent of the
   observed collar defects.

2. **Trusting `is_watertight` as the open/capped test.** Direct trimesh
   cross-section probing of `cargo_sect_shell24_2mm_repaired.stl` at
   increasing Y offsets from `cargo_fwd` (Y=-69.5) and `cargo_aft` (Y=129.0)
   found `is_watertight = True` globally (0 boundary edges) even though these
   faces were supposed to be open per the pipeline's own design comments
   about "OPEN fwd + aft joint faces." This is the point at which the
   watertight-insufficiency finding above was made — `is_watertight` alone
   gave a false "PASS."

3. **A coarse containment probe.** A more sensitive check — `mesh.contains()`
   at 0.05, 0.2, 0.5, and 1.0 mm inside each of the 6 known mating-face
   stations — found that the pre-existing `tools/open_mating_faces.py`'s
   hardcoded 1.0 mm probe depth (from an earlier 2026-07-27 fix) was too
   coarse: 4 of the 6 faces tested "already open" at 1.0 mm but were
   genuinely capped at 0.05-0.5 mm. Only `head_aft` and `middle_fwd` (the
   original two 2026-07-27 fixes) were still genuinely, fully open. This
   confirmed the check itself needed to be more sensitive, not just applied
   more often.

4. **Assuming a one-off fix would hold.** Re-running each owning pipeline
   from clean source, after manually reopening the 4 newly-found capped
   faces, showed each pipeline's own internal repair pass re-capping the
   aperture again on every fresh run — even though that pipeline's own
   internal `verify()`/PASS check reported everything fine. A fix applied
   only to the STL on disk, without changing the pipeline, does not survive
   the next regeneration.

## Solution

`tools/open_mating_faces.py` was refactored from a one-off CLI patch script
into a dual-purpose importable allowlist library, and wired into all three
owning pipelines as the mandatory last step before each writes its final
STL.

**`APERTURES`** — the single source of truth for every known-intentional
mating aperture, keyed by name to match `add_structural_features.MATING_PLANES`
exactly (`tools/open_mating_faces.py:123-148`):

```python
APERTURES = {
    "head_aft": dict(
        rel_path="head_shell24_2mm_repaired.stl",
        method="flatcut", face_y=-71.2, keep_sign=-1, clean_y=None,
    ),
    "middle_fwd": dict(
        rel_path="middle_shell24_2mm_repaired.stl",
        method="plug", face_y=131.0, keep_sign=+1, clean_y=136.0,
    ),
    "cargo_fwd": dict(
        rel_path="cargo/cargo_sect_shell24_2mm_repaired.stl",
        method="plug", face_y=-69.5, keep_sign=+1, clean_y=-63.0,
    ),
    "cargo_aft": dict(
        rel_path="cargo/cargo_sect_shell24_2mm_repaired.stl",
        method="plug", face_y=129.0, keep_sign=-1, clean_y=127.5,
    ),
    "middle_aft": dict(
        rel_path="middle_shell24_2mm_repaired.stl",
        method="plug", face_y=202.3, keep_sign=-1, clean_y=201.0,
    ),
    "rear_fwd": dict(
        rel_path="rear_shell24_2mm_repaired.stl",
        method="plug", face_y=204.3, keep_sign=+1, clean_y=206.0,
    ),
}
```

Each entry declares `method` — `"plug"` (the common case, 5 of 6 faces):
subtract a cavity-shaped plug sized from a nearby clean cross-section
(`clean_y`) to remove a thin membrane at the face; or `"flatcut"` (`head_aft`
only): flat-cut at the first station whose bore is genuinely open, for a
deep rounded closure that a plug would over-cut.

**`is_capped(mesh, face_y, keep_sign, probe=DEFAULT_PROBE, points=None)`**
(`tools/open_mating_faces.py:221-241`) is the point-containment probe. The
default probe depth was lowered from the old hardcoded 1.0 mm to 0.1 mm
(`DEFAULT_PROBE = 0.1` at `tools/open_mating_faces.py:108`). `points`
(each an `(x, z)` pair) defaults to a single legacy probe at the mesh's own
whole-vertex centroid, but see the follow-up fix below — that single-point
default has its own blind spot:

```python
def is_capped(mesh, face_y, keep_sign, probe=DEFAULT_PROBE, points=None):
    """True if the bore is solid `probe` mm inside the mating face (cap present)
    at ANY of `points` (each an (x, z) pair) — capped if any point tests solid."""
    if points is None:
        points = [(mesh.vertices[:, 0].mean(), mesh.vertices[:, 2].mean())]
    probe_pts = [[x, face_y + keep_sign * probe, z] for x, z in points]
    return bool(np.any(mesh.contains(probe_pts)))
```

**`ensure_open(mesh, name, probe=DEFAULT_PROBE)`**
(`tools/open_mating_faces.py:290-365`) is the library entry point every
owning pipeline calls on its own in-memory mesh, for its own aperture(s), as
the very last step before writing the final STL:

- Raises `ValueError` if `name` isn't a known aperture.
- Returns `(mesh, False, "{name}: already open")` immediately if
  `is_capped()` is already `False` — idempotent no-op.
- Otherwise applies the aperture's registered `method` (`open_plug()` or
  `open_flatcut()`), then re-verifies the result: `is_watertight`, exactly 1
  body (`mesh.split(only_watertight=False)`), 0 boundary edges, 0
  non-manifold edges, and `is_capped()` now `False`.
- **Raises `RuntimeError`** — loud, not silent — if that verification fails
  (`tools/open_mating_faces.py:358-362`):

```python
ok = opened.is_watertight and bnd == 0 and nm == 0 and bodies == 1 and not still_capped
if not ok:
    raise RuntimeError(
        f"{name}: reopen attempted but failed verification "
        f"(watertight={opened.is_watertight} bodies={bodies} boundary={bnd} "
        f"nonman={nm} still_capped={still_capped})"
    )
```

`ensure_open()` was wired into all three owning pipelines as an
unconditional tail call, after each pipeline's own internal
repair/finalize pass and before its own `verify()`/export:

- **`merge_cargo_interior.py`**, inside `repair_exported()`, immediately
  after `finalize_watertight()` and before `verify()`/`stamp_export()`
  (`airframe/blender-scripts/merge_cargo_interior.py:1707-1723`):

  ```python
  m = finalize_watertight(m)
  for aperture in ("cargo_fwd", "cargo_aft"):
      m, changed, note = omf.ensure_open(m, aperture)
      print(f"  {note}")
      if changed:
          m.merge_vertices()
  verify(m)  # in-memory result
  stamp_export(m, out_path)
  ```

- **`add_structural_features.py`**, in `main()`'s per-shell loop, via a new
  `SHELL_APERTURES` dict mapping shell name to the aperture names it owns
  (`airframe/blender-scripts/add_structural_features.py:922-925`), applied
  right after `processors[name](mesh)` and before `verify()`
  (`airframe/blender-scripts/add_structural_features.py:972-983`):

  ```python
  SHELL_APERTURES = {
      "head": ("head_aft",),
      "middle": ("middle_fwd", "middle_aft"),
  }
  ...
  modified = processors[name](mesh)
  for aperture in SHELL_APERTURES.get(name, ()):
      modified, changed, note = omf.ensure_open(modified, aperture)
      print(f"  {note}")
  passed = verify(modified, name)
  ```

- **`regen_rear_interior.py`**, in `main()`, right after the boolean
  evaluation and before `verify()`/`stamp_export()`
  (`airframe/blender-scripts/regen_rear_interior.py:228-238`):

  ```python
  out = from_manifold(to_manifold(shell) - neg)
  out = drop_junk(out)
  out, changed, note = omf.ensure_open(out, "rear_fwd")
  print(f"  {note}")
  ok = verify(out, "rear")
  stamp_export(out, OUT)
  ```

## Why This Works

The fix does not try to make any single repair pass smarter about
recognizing "this hole is supposed to be there." That is the wrong layer to
solve it at — `fill_holes()` and a CSG half-space subtraction are both
generic geometric operations with no concept of "mating aperture," and any
attempt to special-case them internally would need to be re-derived for
every future repair technique a pipeline might adopt.

Instead, `ensure_open()` is enforced as an **invariant at write time**,
independent of what any upstream repair step did. No matter how many
internal `fill_holes()` calls a pipeline runs, or how a boolean CSG
subtraction closes off a cut plane, the mandatory tail call re-opens the
known apertures immediately before the mesh reaches disk. This converts a
question of "did the repair pass happen to leave this open" (unverified,
and shown by this bug to be false 4 times out of 6) into "is this aperture
open — checked, and forced if not — right before export" (verified, and
loud on failure via `RuntimeError` rather than silently shipping a bad
part).

The `is_capped()` probe itself works because it tests the actual geometric
property that matters (is there solid material occupying the space just
inside the face) rather than a proxy property (`is_watertight`) that a solid
cap and a genuinely open bore both satisfy identically.

## Prevention

**General principle (apply to any mesh pipeline with an "always repair to
watertight" step):** `trimesh`'s `is_watertight` — and the equivalent
manifoldness check in any other mesh library — cannot distinguish an
intentional open bore from a genuine defect; both report `watertight=True`.
Any pipeline containing a step that unconditionally repairs toward
watertightness (`fill_holes()`, a CSG half-space cut, or similar) needs one
of:

1. An **aperture allowlist enforced at write time** — the pattern
   implemented here: `APERTURES` + `is_capped()` + `ensure_open()` called as
   the unconditional last step of every pipeline that can affect a
   registered aperture, verified before write, loud (`RuntimeError`) on
   failure.
2. Or, at minimum, an **explicit point-containment check** wherever a real
   opening is required by design, run as part of that pipeline's own
   `verify()`/PASS gate — not merely `is_watertight` — so a false "PASS" like
   the one that let this regression run undetected across at least one full
   development cycle cannot recur.

When adding a new mating aperture or a new pipeline that produces one, add
it to `tools/open_mating_faces.py`'s `APERTURES` dict (keying off
`add_structural_features.MATING_PLANES` if it's a hull joint) and call
`ensure_open()` from that pipeline's own finalize step — do not rely on
`tools/open_mating_faces.py`'s CLI `main()` as the enforcement mechanism; per
its own docstring it is now only an ad-hoc audit tool, "NOT the primary
enforcement mechanism" (`tools/open_mating_faces.py:388-389`).

## Follow-up: a partial cap the fix's own verification missed

Shortly after this fix shipped, the project owner reported the head/cargo
joint was "just over half open," with the port side still closed and the
splice collar's profile tracing that same half-cut line instead of following
the joint around. This was a second, distinct bug in the fix above, not a
regression of the original one.

**Root cause:** `cargo_fwd`'s `APERTURES` entry used `clean_y=-68.5` — only
1 mm inside the mating face — to sample the "known-clean" cavity shape for
`open_plug()`'s plug polygon. That station is still inside a near-face taper
zone specific to this joint: direct section probing found the inner-cavity
loop at `Y=-68.5` has bounding box `X[-223.7,-164.7]`, only 45% of the true
full-width cavity (`X[-226.3,-108.6]` by `Y=-63`, confirmed stable at
`X[-227..-232, -103..-110]` out to `Y=-45`) — the loop silently dropped the
port half (less-negative `X`) of the cavity. The plug built from that
station therefore only reopened the starboard half of `cargo_fwd`, leaving
the port side capped.

`ensure_open()`'s own re-verification did not catch this, because
`is_capped()` (before this follow-up) always probed a single point — the
mesh's whole-vertex centroid — and that centroid happened to fall inside the
already-open starboard region. A partial, asymmetric cap can pass a
single-point check as long as the check's one point lands in the open part.

**Fix (`tools/open_mating_faces.py`):**

1. Changed `cargo_fwd`'s `clean_y` from `-68.5` to `-63.0` — confirmed past
   the taper zone (`APERTURES` dict, `tools/open_mating_faces.py:123-148`).
2. Added `_sample_points_in_poly(poly, n=5)` (`tools/open_mating_faces.py:201-217`):
   spreads `n` points across a polygon's X-extent, snapping each to the
   nearest point actually inside the polygon (via `shapely.ops.nearest_points`)
   so every sample is valid even for a concave cavity shape.
3. `is_capped()` now accepts `points` and is capped if **any** point tests
   solid, not just its one implicit centroid (see the updated signature and
   docstring above).
4. `ensure_open()` now derives multi-point coverage for `"plug"` apertures
   from the aperture's own `clean_y` cavity — both for the initial
   capped-check (sampled from the input mesh) and the post-fix
   re-verification (re-sampled from the *opened* mesh, since the pre-fix
   cavity shape may not reflect the post-fix geometry) — at
   `tools/open_mating_faces.py:316-328` (initial) and `350-355` (re-verify).
   `"flatcut"` apertures were left on the single-centroid probe: that method's
   own `first_open_station()` already scans full loop areas across the whole
   cross-section, so it doesn't share this blind spot.

**Verified:** re-running `ensure_open(mesh, "cargo_fwd")` against the
already-"fixed"-but-still-half-capped published STL now correctly reports
`cargo_fwd: was CAPPED — reopened at Y=-69.5 (method=plug)` (previously it
would have silently reported "already open"). Re-running the full
`merge_cargo_interior.py` pipeline from clean source reproduced the same
result. Post-fix section probes at the joint show only small (~100-200 mm²)
residual loops from unrelated nearby features — not the ~8000 mm² capping
loops from before — confirming a genuinely wide-open bore.

This also resolved the previously-flagged head/cargo collar interference: it
was not the separate `WAIST_INSET`/`WAIST_MARGIN` tuning issue guessed at
first — it was a downstream symptom of the same half-open `cargo_fwd`
aperture. Re-running `generate_conforming_collars.py` after this fix shows
all three collars, including head/cargo, at `RESULT: PASS` with `0 mm³`
collar/shell interference (`generate_conforming_collars.py`'s own
`shell_intersection_vol()` check).

**Prevention, extended:** a point-containment check is only as good as its
point. When checking whether a *region* (not a single known point) is
capped, sample multiple points spread across that region's actual expected
footprint — not one point, and not an unrelated proxy point like a whole-mesh
centroid — the same way this fix now derives probe points from the
aperture's own cavity polygon rather than trusting one arbitrary location to
represent the whole opening.

**Still NOT resolved by either fix — do not treat as closed:**

- The cargo/middle joint's cargo-side `fwd_lap` sampling in
  `generate_conforming_collars.py`'s `lap_profile()` still only gets 1
  usable station out of an intended span of roughly a dozen, because the
  CF-thwart pocket at Y=118 — an intentional structural feature, documented
  in `add_structural_features.py`'s `RING_POCKETS["cargo_Y118"]` comment as
  producing "7 disjoint loops" at that cut
  (`add_structural_features.py:310,316`) — overlaps the collar's required
  8 mm insertion-depth sampling window. The result currently reads `PASS`
  (dominated by the narrower middle-side profile) but rests on thin sampling
  data; a design decision (shrink the collar's insertion depth on that side,
  or resize the thwart pocket) is still open and tracked separately from
  this fix.
