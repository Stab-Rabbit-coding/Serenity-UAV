## 0. Design Summary

Each corner is a **canonical articulated leg** — cylinder-cluster thigh,
disc knee, slotted shin, disc ankle, tri-pad arrowhead foot — that **pivots
as one rigid printed frame about an M3 stainless hip pin** inside a
hull-flank bay. Two **spring** bowed wires (elastic, recoverable) and two
**ductile** bowed wires (plastic, sacrificial fuse) span the hip joint at a
6 mm bellcrank radius: leg flexion (foot swinging up-outboard) compresses
each wire along its chord so its pre-formed bow deepens — mechanically
identical to Rev R5. What the articulation adds is a **lever between foot
and wires** (65 : 6 for the 3.0in leg, 41.9 : 6 for the compact 1.5in leg),
so millimetres of wire stroke become tens of millimetres of hull settle:

| Quantity | Rev R5 (rigid post) | **Rev R6, 1.5in (default)** | **Rev R6, 3.0in (extended)** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Peak decel, 6 ft tail-down / level | ≈1,000 g | **81 g / 162 g** | **52 g / 104 g** |
| Hull settle absorbing the drop | ≈1.8 mm | **22.6 mm** (of 38.1 mm) | **35.1 mm** (of 80 mm) |
| Ground clearance | 31 mm (1.22 in) | **38.1 mm (1.50 in)** | **80.0 mm (3.15 in)** |
| Leg appearance | Thingiverse blade derivative | Canonical articulated leg, canonical bay stations | (same, longer leg) |

Both variants reuse the **same wire hardware** (solved once against the
3.0in lever, §4.7) and the **same bay/foot** — only the printed leg-frame
length differs, so the shorter lever of the compact leg trades ground
clearance for a higher peak deceleration. Both are still far below the Rev
R5 rigid post's ≈1,000 g.

An M3 **extension-stop cross-pin** through the bay clevis ears carries the
opposite rotation (leg hanging under gravity, in-flight loads); landing
loads never touch it — they always run foot → hip pin + wires.

SCAD sources: `airframe/openscad/fuselage/canonical_leg_r6_1_5in.scad`
(default) and `canonical_leg_r6_3_0in.scad` (extended) — identical part
library, different `KNEE`/`ANKLE`/`GROUND_Z`/`R_H` skeleton constants.
STLs: `airframe/stls/fuselage/landing-gear/lg_r6_1_5in_*.stl`,
`lg_r6_3_0in_*.stl`, and `lg_r6_common_*.stl` for the shared bay/foot/wire
parts (§16).

## 1. Purpose and Requirements

The landing gear system must:

1. **Survive a vertical drop test onto hard level ground at design AUW**
   without cracking the hull shell. Height: **6 ft (1.829 m) baseline; a
   4 ft (1.219 m) reduction is under consideration** — §4.4 carries both
   schedules; the difference is wire stock diameter only. **Decision open:
   LG-17.**
2. **Absorb lateral loads** from not-quite-vertical landings (±15° design case).
3. **Fail internally, progressively, not catastrophically** — overload must
   produce defined, observable, partial degradation that still leaves the
   aircraft supported.
4. **Be field-replaceable** with common hand tools.
5. **Provide 3-D lateral stability** (tipping resistance both axes).
6. *(new in R6)* **Match the canonical Serenity landing gear** in form and
   in bay placement ([REF-CAD-003] Sheet 5; [REF-CAD-002]), deviating only
   where function demands (§2.4).
7. *(corrected 2026-07-23)* **Ground clearance is an aircraft-safety
   minimum, not a cargo-clearance spec.** The 3×3×4 ft cargo is carried
   internally; the aircraft lands *over* a ground-staged box with the cargo
   doors open and winches it in, so the gear does not need to straddle the
   box's height. Extra height beyond what flight safety needs (avoiding a
   belly/tail strike, keeping the hull clear of ground debris through the
   absorption stroke) is not required. This replaces the ≥76 mm (3.0 in)
   payload-mission requirement carried since Rev R1 — see §4.7 for both
   the resulting 1.5 in default and the 3.0 in option kept for rough-field
   use.

The landing gear does NOT need to be reusable after a worst-case drop
(ductile wires are consumables).

### 2.3 Load Share — the CG Correction

Build CG is at hull-frame **Y = +111.5 mm** (nacelle pivot = canonical
"Engine Pivots 360°" balance centre). Against the canonical wheelbase this
is **86% aft**:

```text
Static share: aft leg 43.1% each, fore leg 6.9% each
```

Canon agrees — the real ship is aft-heavy on its gear and additionally
deploys a "Cargo Bay Landing Pad" (Sheet 5 callout); on this build the
middle-section rear skids play that role (tip-back backstop; §10).
Tip-back margin about the aft-feet line: ≈12° to skid contact.
**Wire sizing is driven by the aft-leg worst case, one common schedule for
all corners** (fore legs simply carry large margins).

### 2.4 Canonical Form — Kept and Deviated

Kept (per [REF-CAD-003] Sheet 5 detail + [REF-CAD-002]): telescoping
cylinder-cluster thigh at ~53°, disc knee and ankle joints with the
4-satellite-hole wheel pattern, slotted flat shin, tri-pad arrowhead foot
with ribbed treads, recessed flank bays, "Feet Pivot 90°" (realized as a
square ankle spigot indexing the foot at 90° steps).

Deviations (functional, deliberate):

1. **Leg length** — canonical proportion gives ≈11 mm belly clearance at
   24-in scale. Requirement 7 (corrected 2026-07-23) sizes the gear to an
   aircraft-safety minimum, not a cargo-clearance spec, hence the two
   offered lengths: **1.5 in (default)** and **3.0 in (extended, kept)**.
2. **Articulation lives at the hip only** — knee/ankle discs are styling on
   a one-piece printed leg frame (print lying on its side: swing-plane
   bending stays in the layer plane).
3. **Fixed-down gear** — no retraction; the bay plate supplies the
   canonical recess look.

## 4. Mechanism and Wire Sizing

### 4.1 Bowed-Wire Model (carried from Rev R5, unchanged)

A straight wire with one shallow mid-span bow (rise h₀ = 3.5 mm), loaded
axially along its chord, collapses by deepening the bow via a hinge at the
crown (2-hinge model):

```text
P = 2M/h₀       M_plastic = σ_f d³/6      (ductile, flow stress σ_f = 550 MPa)
                M_elastic = σ_w π d³/32   (spring, working stress σ_w = 900 MPa)
stroke s = (h_f² − h₀²)/(2B)              (B = bow span; validity cap h_f ≤ 0.35 B)
U = P·s                                    (constant-force plateau approximation)
```

Wire ends stay **straight** (socket seat depth + 2 mm run-in per end) so
they seat in straight bores; only the exposed span B carries the bow.
Stock length L = B + 2·(seat + 2 mm). This wire schedule is identical for
both leg-length variants — see §4.7.

### 4.2 The Hip Lever — Two Variants, One Wire Set

Wires act at bellcrank radius **r = 6 mm** about the hip pin in both
variants; the foot's ground force acts at horizontal arm **R_h**, which is
the one parameter that changes between variants:

```text
Plateau ground force per leg   F = 2 P r / R_h        (ductile pair engaged)
Hull settle                    d = U_leg / F
Wire stroke                    s = d · r / R_h  =  U_wire / P   (R_h-independent)
```

Because stroke s = U_wire/P has no R_h term, **the wire itself does the
same job in either leg** — but the resulting ground force F, hull settle
d, and peak deceleration all scale with R_h. The wire schedule below is
solved once against the **3.0in lever (design point)**; §4.7 shows what
the *same hardware* delivers when reused in the 1.5in leg.

### 4.3 Spring Pair (elastic phase — solved at the 3.0in design point)

```text
Sized to absorb the ordinary-hard-landing energy fully elastically:
  per-leg elastic capacity 3.51 J (per wire 1.755 J)
  d = 3.35 mm, bow span 23 mm, stock L = 37 mm, h₀ = 3.5 mm
  P = 1,896 N at elastic limit; stroke 0.93 mm (avail 1.14, 1.2× reserve)
  mass 2.56 g × 8 = 20.5 g/aircraft (both variants — shared hardware)
```

Reused in each variant (§4.7): **3.0in** — 350 N/leg elastic limit, 10.0 mm
settle at limit, 46 g onset (level). **1.5in** — 543 N/leg elastic limit,
6.5 mm settle at limit, 71 g onset (level).

### 4.4 Ductile Pair — the 6 ft vs 4 ft Decision (LG-17)

Both drop-height schedules share **bow span 55 mm, stock L = 75 mm,
h₀ = 3.5 mm, and every printed part unchanged** — the choice is wire stock
diameter only, solved at the 3.0in design point:

| | **6 ft schedule (baseline)** | **4 ft schedule** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Worst-leg energy (tail-down) | 28.1 J | 18.7 J |
| Ductile wire diameter | **Ø4.36 mm** | **Ø3.81 mm** |
| Wire collapse force P | 4,333 N | 2,889 N |
| Wire mass ×8 | 70.2 g | 53.6 g |
| 3.0in plateau force / settle / decel (T-D / level) | 800 N / 35.1 mm / 52 / 104 g | 533 N / 35.1 mm / 35 / 70 g |
| 1.5in plateau force / settle / decel (T-D / level) | 1,241 N / 22.6 mm / 81 / 162 g | 827 N / 22.6 mm / 54 / 108 g |
| 6 ft drop capability | 1.0× (design point) | **not covered** (0.67×) |
| 4 ft drop capability | 1.5× margin | 1.0× (design point) |

Decision guidance: the 6 ft schedule flown and **tested at 4 ft**
demonstrates a 1.5× energy margin — arguably the best of both, though the
1.5in variant's residual clearance margin (§4.7) is worth weighing before
committing. Choosing the 4 ft schedule trades away 6 ft survivability for
lower peak deceleration and less wire mass in both variants. Either way
the printed geometry is frozen — **LG-15 procurement is the only gate.**

### 4.5 Stroke Reserve and Fired Shape

Full stroke uses 3.24 mm of the 3.26 mm model-validity reserve (identical
in both variants, since stroke is R_h-independent, §4.2); the fired
ductile bow rises to ≈19 mm over the 55 mm span — a **visibly bent,
unambiguous field-inspection indicator**
(`lg_r6_common_ductile_wire_deformed.stl`). Hip rotation at full stroke is
30.9° in both variants. During full flex the foot skids outboard (more so
in the shorter-radius 1.5in leg); TPU treads slide rather than grab —
intended.

### 4.6 Structure Checks — R_h-Independent Part (both variants)

The hip bending moment **M = 2·P·r depends only on the wire hardware**,
not on R_h — so the leg-frame thigh section margin is **identical in both
variants**:

```text
Leg frame @ hip:  M = 52.0 N·m; section 2×Ø14 mm cylinders @ 18 mm ctrs + web
                  Z = 2,220 mm³ → σ = 23.4 MPa vs 27.5 MPa allowable
                  (allowable = CF-PETG yield/2) → 1.17× on top of 2× = 2.3× total
Bay boss bearing: Ø11 boss / wire bore, 70 MPa → 6,300 N vs P = 4,333 N → 1.45×
```

Leg frame print: CF-PETG, **100% infill**, 0.15 mm layers, **lying on the
−Y face** so swing-plane bending stays in the layer plane (both variants).

### 4.7 Two-Variant Comparison — Full Numbers

Full matrix at the 6 ft ductile schedule, generated by
`tools/landing_gear_r6_sizing.py`:

| Quantity | **1.5in (default)** | **3.0in (extended)** |
| --- | --- | --- |

| Lever ratio R_h : r | 6.98 : 1 | 10.83 : 1 |
| Belly clearance | 38.1 mm (1.50 in) | 80.0 mm (3.15 in) |
| Ground force F_leg | 1,241 N (279 lbf) | 800 N (180 lbf) |

| Peak decel, tail-down / level | **81 g / 162 g** | **52 g / 104 g** |
| Hip pin double-shear margin | 3.4× | 5.3× |
| Bay boss bearing margin | 1.45× (shared hardware) | 1.45× (shared hardware) |
| Lateral ±15° load into hip pin | 333 N/leg | 214 N/leg |
| Static stance load / wire | 23.1 N | 35.8 N |
| Thigh bending margin | 1.17× (identical, §4.6) | 1.17× (identical, §4.6) |

**Safety note on the "either wire alone" fallback (§6, level 3):** if one
of the two ductile wires fails to engage, the surviving wire alone must
absorb roughly double the settle. For the **3.0in** leg this still fits
(70 mm of 80 mm clearance). For the **1.5in** leg it does **not**
(45 mm needed vs 38.1 mm available) — the compact variant has no margin
for a single-wire-only arrest. This is the primary engineering trade of
choosing the compact default; LG-13 (wire retention) and LG-14
(instrumented test) carry extra weight for the 1.5in variant as a result.

The compact 1.5in leg is ≈67 g lighter than the 3.0in leg (shorter thigh

gear-mass centroid's height only (shorter legs carry their mass closer to
the hip); it is symmetric fore/aft and does not shift the aircraft's X/Y
CG, only trims Z (vertical) CG height slightly upward for the 1.5in
variant. No verified precision claim beyond that is made here.

variant, ≤300 g for 1.5in) — candidate cuts: bay plate lightening,
shin/collar slimming, hollow thigh with CF tube insert. Until closed, the
AUW ledger must carry the growth above and LG-15 must re-check wire Ø
against as-weighed AUW (d ∝ AUW^⅓).

## 13. Assembly Procedure

1. **Choose the leg variant** (1.5in default or 3.0in extended, §4.7) — all
   other steps are identical either way.
2. **Bays:** bolt 4 bay plates to the cargo-shell flanks at the §2.2
   stations (4× M3 + internal backing plates each; LG-02 conforming detail).
3. **Wires:** form 16 + spares per §11.2/11.3 on the bending jig (straight
   ends, mid-span bow); temper check per LG-15/LG-16.
4. **Leg:** seat foot on spigot (choose 90° index), install M2.5 cross-bolt.
5. **Hang:** set leg lug between clevis ears, drive hip pin; install
   extension-stop cross-pin; leg should hang freely against the stop.
6. **Wires in:** seat each wire leg-end in its bellcrank socket and bay-end
   in its boss (spring outers first), apply LG-13 retention.
7. **Safety cord:** foot hub → bay → hull anchor.
8. Repeat ×4; verify all four feet touch on a flat plate (Z-level rule:
   shim to the lowest foot). **Do not mix variants across corners.**

## 14. Field Replacement (after a hard landing)

1. Inspect: fired ductile wires show a deep (~19 mm) bow — replace both on
   any fired corner. Spring wires must show zero set — replace if bent.
2. Release LG-13 retention, withdraw fired wires, seat replacements
   (cut-to-length stock + bend jig, or pre-formed spares — same stock for
   either leg variant).
3. Inspect leg frame, pins, bay bosses (no yield expected, §4.6);
   check foot spigot bolt.

**Tools:** 2 mm and 2.5 mm hex keys, pin punch, wire bend jig, side cutters.
